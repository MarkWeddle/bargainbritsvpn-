THE THREE THINGS YOU'RE EXPOSED TO

It's rarely about "hackers." It's about three very specific people and organisations seeing more than they should.

MONEY
Shared WiFi you don't control

Hotel and airport networks are shared with everyone in the building, and many have no password at all. Anyone can set up a network with a convincing name like "Airport_Free_WiFi" and see which sites the devices joining it connect to. Most banking apps encrypt their own traffic, but a VPN puts everything else on your phone or laptop behind the same encryption.

IDENTITY
An identity theft mess is harder to sort from abroad

Sorting out identity fraud means calls to banks, replacing cards and documents, and disputing charges. That's hard enough from your own sofa — and harder from a hotel room in a different time zone.

PRIVACY
Your broadband provider, logging where you go — by law

Under the UK's Investigatory Powers Act, your internet provider can be required to keep a record of every site and service you connect to for up to 12 months, timestamped and tied to your account. A VPN encrypts that traffic and hides your IP address, so what gets logged is a Surfshark server — not your evening.
